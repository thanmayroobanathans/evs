How the adaptive engine works

Kalman filter with guess and slip. Each of the 30 topics has a hidden knowledge level (log-odds m, uncertainty P). The measurement model is P(correct) = 0.25 + 0.73·σ(m): a 25% guess floor for 4 options and a 2% careless-slip rate. Because guessing makes a correct answer weaker evidence, a miss moves the estimate more than a hit. Process noise lets P grow on topics you have left alone.

Thompson sampling for topics. Each step draws a plausible knowledge level from every topic's posterior and picks the topic with the most sampled marks at risk, (1 − σ(m̃))·√share. Uncertain topics win some draws, and weak ones win most.

Half-life forgetting per question. Predicted recall is 2^(−lag/h). A question returns when recall falls to 90%. A correct answer multiplies the half-life h by 2.2, and a miss resets it to a short value. Topics you know well stretch the gaps further.

Trap queue. About 50 questions hinge on conflicting numbers across your notes, time horizons (20 vs 100 years) or look-alike facts (Act sections, Scope 1/2/3, Nitrosomonas vs Nitrobacter). Any question you miss by misreading, mixing up facts, or a number slip joins them. Trap questions need two correct answers in a row to count as mastered. Key words in each question are highlighted so you don't misread them.

Score prediction. Each topic's chance of scoring, guesses included, is weighted by its share of the syllabus. The range is an 80% interval covering both exam luck and model uncertainty.

Chance of 40/40. 3,000 simulated papers. Each one draws a knowledge level for every topic from its posterior, then each answer from the 40-question blueprint. With a 2% slip on every question, even perfect knowledge caps the chance of 40/40 near 45% (0.9840). The rest comes down to checking your answers on the day.
